V3RDE Open the app

Extra food goes to people near it, not to the trash

V3RDE connects people who have extra food with people near them who need a meal. Posted food is open to grab for 30 minutes. If nobody picks it up, V3RDE finds a food program near the food. To post, a giver takes one photo, and the app makes a listing for each item in it.

Who uses V3RDE

Three kinds of users. Each one has a different job.

Give

Share your food

A person or a business with food that nobody will eat.

  • Take one photo: one item or a full shelf.
  • Check the list. Speak or type to change it.
  • Post. Each listing is live for 30 minutes.
  • Nobody took it? Tap Rescue.

Receive

Find food near you

A person who wants a meal from food near them.

  • See food within 1, 5 or 10 miles.
  • Grab an item. You get the pickup place and the directions.
  • Cannot go? Release it, so another person can grab it.

Your place is not stored. Distances show to 0.1 mile.

Food programs

Take what nobody grabbed

Food banks and pantries take shelf-stable food. Soup kitchens and meal programs take perishable food.

  • The app finds the places nearest to the food.
  • Small amount: the giver takes it to one of the places. Call before you go.
  • Large amount: the giver sends a short message first, from their own email.

The message asks if the program can take the food. It does not say the food is safe.

How it works

From one photo to a meal, in 7 steps.

  1. 1

    Take a photo

    One item, a tray or a full shelf.

  2. 2

    Every item is found

    Each one gets a name and a count. Items show as they come in.

  3. 3

    Make the photo better

    Better light or a plain background. The food does not change.

  4. 4

    Talk to change it

    "The salad is gone. Pick up at the back door."

  5. 5

    Post

    Each item is a listing for 30 minutes.

  6. 6

    Grab and pick up

    A person near you grabs it and comes to get it.

  7. 7

    Rescue

    Nobody came? A food program near the food gets it.

How it is built

Fast checks run on our own server. The Meta models do the hard work in the cloud. A photo that is not food stops on our server and costs nothing.

Your phone or browser

One app for the web, iPhone and Android.

Camera and barcode

The barcode is read on the device.

Voice clip

Made on the device as a WAV file.

Your place

Location or ZIP code. A receiver's place is not stored. A giver's place is kept rounded and is not shown.

Our server (the edge)

One small ARM server. Each part is its own Go service.

Food gate: PE-Core-S16-384

A Meta model on our server. It says food or not food before any paid call.

Food DB

A barcode hit fills the listing at once.

Sign-in, listings, distance

The 30-minute timer, grab and release.

Ledger

Each model call, with its time, tokens and cost.

Meta's cloud

The Meta Model API, plus Muse Spark Open.

SAM 3.1

Finds and counts each item: food, package and tray.

Muse Spark 1.3

Names the items. Checks edits. Turns speech into changes. Writes the rescue message.

Muse Image 1.0

Makes the photo better.

Muse Voice Transcribe 1.0

Turns the voice clip into text.

Muse Spark Open

Translates the app text into other languages. In progress.

What happens to one photo

  1. The barcode is looked up first. A hit is the fast path.
  2. The food gate and SAM 3.1 start at the same time. The paid naming call waits for the gate.
  3. SAM 3.1 finds every item. Muse Spark 1.3 reads the photo with a number drawn on each item, and names them all in one call.
  4. Items that are the same are grouped and counted. Small items get a "check this" mark.
  5. Muse Image 1.0 makes the photo better. Then Muse Spark 1.3 compares the two photos. If one item, label or count changed, the original stays.
  6. Code checks every change that a model proposes. Voice cannot add food that the photo does not show.

It learns from the givers

The app records each name and count that a giver changes. These labels tune the size limit for the "check this" mark. A person approves each change.

It is safe to leave running

Item photos are for their owner only. Each caller has a rate limit and a daily cost limit. Every change comes with tests, and every design decision names its source.
