### D357. The mark and the wordmark (T1 + G), from a research rubric
- https://doi.org/10.1177/002224299806200202 — Henderson & Cote (1998), "Guidelines for Selecting or Modifying Logos": natural, harmony, moderate elaboration and repetition make a logo easier to recognise and liked; the rubric's criteria 1–4. T1 repeats one V three times (the 3 of V3RDE).
- https://westminsterresearch.westminster.ac.uk/item/qqv8q/let-the-logo-do-the-talking-the-influence-of-logo-descriptiveness-on-brand-equity — Luffarelli et al. (2019): a descriptive logo (it says what the brand does) helps brand equity; the rubric's criterion 5 (the weak point of every option: no food signal; the leaf of the wordmark is the one food signal).
- https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8436 — Cian, Krishna & Elder (2014), "This Logo Moves Me": a static logo that implies motion makes the brand seem modern and engaging; the stepped depth of the three V's.
- https://repository.uwl.ac.uk/id/eprint/7368/ — Lelis et al. (2020), "Typography: the constant vector of dynamic logos": a fixed type element anchors a logo that changes; the wordmark G stays the same next to the mark.
- https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucv049 — Jiang et al. (2016): circular and angular logo shapes signal soft and hard brand attributes (noted, not scored).

### D358. One source for the geometry: the `logo` tokens
- https://github.com/software-mansion/react-native-svg — the mark and the leaf are `Polygon` and `Path` in an `Svg` with a `viewBox`, so one set of path units draws every size. The polygon, the two depths (8, 16), the leaf path, the leaf size (0.36 em), its gap (0.04 em) and the icon share (0.46) are `logo` tokens in `theme.ts`, listed in the style guide, so `style.test.mts` passes with no exception (the mockup had them as numbers in its code: 2 of 9 style checks failed there). The colours are the palette tokens (no new colour); the About and Learn pages use their CSS colour tokens. `brand.test.mts` fails when a layer, a depth, a size or a page's copy of the polygon breaks.

### D359. The leaf sits on the baseline (the Poppins metrics)
- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/spec/hhea — the ascender and descender of the `hhea` table set the line box. The shipped Poppins files (`@expo-google-fonts/poppins`, 400 and 600; https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Poppins) have unitsPerEm 1000, hhea ascent 1050, descent -350 (read with fontTools from `Poppins_600SemiBold.ttf`). In a line of height L the glyph box is centred, so the baseline is L/2 - (1050 - 350)/2/1000 x font size above the line bottom: `logo.baselineEm` 0.35. On the web pages the leaf is a flex item aligned to the baseline (an SVG's bottom edge is its baseline).

### D361. The app icons: full bleed, centred, made by a committed tool
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons.md — "The system masks all layer edges to produce an icon's final shape. For iOS, iPadOS, and macOS icons, provide square layers so the system can apply rounded corners"; "Keep primary content centered". The icon is a full-bleed `accent` square, the mark `logo.iconShare` (46 %, as the Hungree "H" icon, 470/1024) of the side, centred on its box (T9: no optical shift).
- https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/config/app/ — `icon`, `android.adaptiveIcon` (foreground, background, monochrome images) and `web.favicon` name the files; `app.json` does not change, only the files.
- https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/launch/icon_design_adaptive — "Size all layers to 108x108 dp"; "The outer 18 dp on each of the four sides of the layers is reserved for masking", so 72 of 108 dp show (`logo.adaptiveVisible` 2/3); the logo "must not exceed 66x66 dp, because the inner 66x66 dp of the icon appears within the masked viewport"; a monochrome layer for themed icons. The foreground has the mark at 46 % of the visible part, so it looks as `icon.png`; the Go test checks that every pixel of the foreground and monochrome layers is inside the 66 dp circle. The monochrome layer is the three V's in one colour (T9: "merged stack"), white as the earlier file.
- https://pkg.go.dev/golang.org/x/image/vector — the tool (`mobile/tools/brand`, Go, T9's choice) fills the polygons with this anti-aliasing rasteriser; the repo already uses `golang.org/x/image` v0.36.0 (inference, post), so no new package. It reads the tokens and the four palette colours from `theme.ts`. https://pkg.go.dev/image/png — an opaque image is written as RGB, so the 5 opaque files keep the colour type of the earlier files; the two Android layers are RGBA. `splash-icon.png` stays byte-equal to `icon.png`.
