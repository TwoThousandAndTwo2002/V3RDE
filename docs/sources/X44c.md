### D173. One call for the whole review list, when the scan ends
- https://docs.aws.amazon.com/apigateway/latest/developerguide/http-api-quotas.html — 30 s per request through API Gateway: the call only starts the jobs (202) and the app polls each one, as for one touch-up.
- A touch-up takes 21 s to 27 s (X42a, X42f). If the app starts it when the scan ends, the edit is ready while the donor reads the list. With one call per photo, the third photo of a scan got `503 busy` (2 slots) and the fifth `429` (4 per minute). One batch call is one hit of the rate limit; the daily caps still count every job (X44a), so a batch cannot spend more than single calls.

### D174. A short queue instead of 503 busy
- https://pkg.go.dev/time#NewTimer — the wait for a slot is a `select` on the slot and a timer, so a job never waits for ever.
- The box has 2 GiB, so the slots stay 2 (X36a). A batch job waits for a free slot (`queued`). The queue holds at most 12 jobs of all callers and each waits at most 3 minutes (2 slots x 27 s x 6 rounds is under 3 minutes); then the job ends `failed` and its hold is freed with nothing paid. The single route keeps its 503 `busy`.

### D175. Every photo is checked before any job
- https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0xa1-broken-object-level-authorization/ — "check if the logged-in user has access to perform the requested action on the record."
- Each `photo_id` must be an item photo of the caller (the X36a and X42e rules: `/api` the session's photos, `/v1` the token's photos). One that is not refuses the whole call with the same 404 as an unknown id, before any reservation, so a batch cannot be used to probe ids. At most 6 ids (a scan's review list), no repeated id, no body.
